Welcome back to the show. Today I want to talk about how small creators can grow faster in a world full of artificial intelligence tools. I have spent the last two years testing almost every tool out there, and I learned a few things the hard way.

Here is the first surprising thing. Most people think the algorithm rewards long videos. That is simply not true anymore. When I cut my ten minute videos into short clips of thirty to forty seconds, my reach went up by almost five times in a single month. The reason is simple. People decide in the first two seconds whether they will keep watching. If your opening line is weak, nothing else matters. So every clip needs a hook, a strong first sentence that makes people curious.

Let me tell you a quick story. Last year I posted a video about saving money on software. It got maybe two hundred views. A week later, I took one sentence from that same video, where I said that I was paying for eleven subscriptions I never used, and I made that the first line of a short clip. That clip got three hundred thousand views. Same content, same person, same idea. The only difference was where the video started. That day I understood that editing is not about cutting. Editing is about choosing.

Now, the second lesson is about captions. Around eighty percent of people watch short videos with the sound off, especially in offices, in buses and late at night. If your video has no captions, you are invisible to most of your audience. But captions should not just be text on the screen. The best creators highlight one word at a time, so the eyes keep moving and the brain keeps reading. It feels like the video is talking directly to you.

The third lesson is the one nobody likes to hear. Consistency beats quality. I know that sounds strange. But a creator who posts a good clip every single day will almost always beat a creator who posts a perfect video once a month. The algorithm needs data about you, and your audience needs a habit. You cannot build a habit with one video a month.

So how do you post every day without burning out? This is where artificial intelligence actually helps. Not by replacing your creativity, but by removing the boring work. Finding the best moments in a long podcast used to take me three hours. Writing captions took another hour. Cropping the video so my face stays in the center of a vertical frame took even longer. Today, tools can do the first draft of all of that in a few minutes, and I spend my time on the part that matters, which is the idea and the story.

One more thing before we finish. Do not chase every new tool. Pick one simple workflow and stick with it for ninety days. Record one long conversation a week, turn it into five or six short clips, and post one clip every day. Track which hooks work, and do more of those. After three months, you will know your audience better than any course could teach you.

That is it for today. If you remember only one thing, remember this. Your first sentence decides everything. Make it count. Thank you for listening, and I will see you in the next episode.
